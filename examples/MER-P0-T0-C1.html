Framing
The Meridian Labs take-home requires submitting the AI chat logs alongside the deliverables. Raw transcripts satisfy the letter of that requirement but not its intent — nobody reads twelve conversations, and the reviewer's actual question is where the human judgment sat. The problem was how to produce readable process documentation without spending a full day cleaning up transcripts after the work was already done.
Decision
Set up a per-task checkpoint card generated at the end of each session, capturing framing, decision, AI gap, judgment call, naming, and output, with a link to the source log. Rejected writing a process summary at the end (recall degrades, and the specific moments of correction are exactly what gets forgotten first) and rejected annotating the transcripts inline (still requires the reviewer to read them).
AI gap
The first schema Claude proposed had a "lessons learned" row, which produced generic reflection on every test card — the kind of content that reads as filler. Cut it and replaced it with "AI gap", which forces a specific, checkable claim instead of a sentiment.
Judgment call
Added an explicit instruction that an empty AI gap must be stated as empty rather than filled with a soft critique. The failure mode of a self-reported process log is manufactured self-awareness, and a reviewer spots it immediately. One honest "no significant gap" makes the other eleven cards credible.
